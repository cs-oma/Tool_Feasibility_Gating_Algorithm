The Core Conflict

Individual Rationality vs. Collective Stability

In a shared environment, agents must choose between powerful (Slow) tools and efficient (Fast) tools.

S

The "SLOW" Tool (Defect)

High individual utility (better answers), but high latency. If everyone chooses this, the queue explodes, deadlines are missed, and nobody gets anything (Utility = 0).
F

The "FAST" Tool (Cooperate)

Moderate individual utility, but low latency. Using this frees up bandwidth for others. It represents "Nice" behavior in Axelrod's terms.

Comparative Analysis

Performance Benchmarks

Why the "Axelrod" approach (Win-Stay, Lose-Shift) outperforms Greedy strategies in high-load scenarios.

Deadline Miss Rate (Lower is Better)

While the Greedy strategy hits nearly 100% failure rate under load, the Axelrod agent adapts, keeping failure rates comparable to the theoretical Global Oracle.

1. Adaptive Stability

The simulation proves that decentralized agents using Win-Stay, Lose-Shift logic can approximate the stability of a central controller without the communication overhead.

2. The Price of Anarchy

The gap between the "Greedy" bar and the "Oracle" bar represents the Price of Anarchy. The Axelrod agent minimizes this gap by voluntarily sacrificing individual tool quality (Slow -> Fast) to preserve system throughput.

3. Pareto Efficiency

While the "Cooperative" strategy is safe, it under-utilizes the system when traffic is low. Axelrod agents are superior because they defect (use slow tools) when the queue is empty, maximizing utility until congestion signals them to cooperate.
